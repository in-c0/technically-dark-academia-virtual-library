# TDA — Visual Direction
**Technically Dark Academia** · *Technology IS magic.*

Status: **art-direction exploration, not verified gameplay art.**
Owner of implementation and release: development team. This public document supplies creative references, copy boundaries, and review criteria.

## North star
Make the *feeling of wandering into a real bookshop* possible from anywhere: airiness, quietly coexisting readers, unexpected discoveries through tangible covers and shelf organization, the suggestion that every title opens another life. Classic academia contributes craft and ritual; future technology contributes agency, access, and discovery. The user should never feel trapped in a gloomy fantasy library.

## Three visual lanes

### A. The Grand Bookshop — aspirational TDA world
- Historic architecture, expressive scale, glass ceilings, brass armillary sphere as the brand motif.
- Lived-in social warmth: browsing figures whose presence is subtle, not staged crowd spectacle.
- Warm reading lamps versus cool rainy dusk; richly layered shelves and navigable aisles.
- Use the concept image as atmospheric inspiration; its impossible architecture is **not** a build specification or proof of implemented VR/3D content.

### B. The Everyday Bookshop — product experience anchor
- Familiar independent bookshop at human scale: tables crowded with new discoveries, clear categories, mixed cover colors, staff-selected books, a little greenery.
- The pleasure comes from genuine books and serendipitous wandering, not from excessive fantasy decoration.
- Prefer relaxed medium-width aisles and readable cover-level interaction over cinematic spectacle.
- This visual lane should anchor the earliest playable experience.

### C. Living Pages — reading imagination
- Physical book and notes remain grounded and legible.
- An optional interpretive layer expands the current passage into references, diagrams, or an imagined scene.
- Clearly distinguish source material, historical evidence, player annotations, and AI interpretation.
- The visual is speculative feature art, **not** a game screenshot and not a promise of generated 3D content at launch.

## Palette and material relationships
- Ink / deep wood: #1B2326; dark bottle green: #263B35; oxblood accent: #612F3B.
- Paper and skin: #E9DFC9; lamp brass: #BB945E; rainy blue: #547385.
- Actual books supply chromatic diversity. A shelf of monochromatic fake spines would destroy the central browsing experience.
- Marble, oak, paper grain, leather, woven textiles, brushed brass, glass reflections—used with physical plausibility.
- Text must remain accessible; do not force low-contrast tiny serif fonts in functional controls.

## Composition and interaction
1. Establish atmosphere on entry, then make books the most visually discoverable objects.
2. Let covers invite curiosity before requiring search.
3. Prioritize meaningful shelf-level categories (staff picks, new arrivals, biology, literature, engineering, philosophy, art) with visible variety.
4. A selected book opens an actionable reader panel: metadata, permitted preview, save, external link, notes, related material.
5. Persistent spatial anchor: leaving an expanded passage returns the user to the same book and shelf position.
6. Presence is independent of availability to talk: who I see, who sees me, who can message me are distinct permissions.
7. Browse / Study / Research / Do Not Disturb / user-defined modes change environment and controls without losing reading context.

## Quality and rights guardrails
- No hand-coded placeholder 3D or simplistic procedural geometry to stand in for professional environmental art.
- Do not invent bookstore partnerships, actual store inventory, live users, or live store footage.
- Covers and thumbnails from external catalogs must be used in accordance with their terms and licenses; avoid generating fake text that masquerades as real book metadata.
- Public catalog descriptions and snippets must preserve provenance. Quotations require their own permissions and attribution.
- Visual acceptance requires actual renders, close inspection, representative device tests, and truthful distinction between concept and shipped capabilities.
